Imagine going into the airport, and you are no longer asked to show your ID, but instead are asked to show your face to a camera, which decides whether or not you are allowed to pass through security. That is how facial recognition and AI surveillance systems work, and they are becoming way more common in places like airports, schools, stores, and police investigations around the world. AI surveillance and biometric identification use various features such as facial recognition, fingerprints, and other biometric data to identify people. These systems can improve society and make identification way faster, but they also raise concerns when it comes to privacy, discrimination, and false identification. Although AI surveillance and biometric identification systems can improve safety and make identification easier, it is necessary for there to be strict laws, and multiple verification checks to prevent innocent people from being harmed and falsely identified.

Through expansion of technology, there has been easier access to information and processing. It is what AI surveillance is meant to do through its function to monitor, track, and analyze people's activities. Biometric identification which is similar to AI surveillance uses features such as facial recognition, fingerprints, and eye scans to identify people. Although these systems are designed to make identification faster and more accurate, they also raise concerns about privacy and security.

AI surveillance can improve safety and make the security process way more efficient. One example of this being applied can be seen in the Transportation Security Administration's new TSA PreCheck Touchless ID program. The program uses facial recognition technology to verify the identity of the person travelling without requiring them to show their physical ID. In the article "TSA's Faster PreCheck Lane Is Expanding to More Airports," TSA spokesperson R. Carter Langston explained that passengers appreciate the system more because of its “speed” and “efficiency” and that the travelers can simply just show their face and be allowed through the checkpoint (Compton 2026).  This article shows how biometric identification can make the travel process way faster and convenient for the people. Instead of spending time searching for identification documents, travelers are able to move through security lines way more quickly while still having their identity verified to ensure safety. As a result, AI surveillance systems can improve efficiency in places that require large amounts of security screening.

The TSA program also demonstrates how AI surveillance can improve the overall travel experience. Airports process thousands of passengers every day, and the long security line can create lots of delays and frustration. By using facial recognition, airports are able to reduce wait time significantly and improve efficiency while also maintaining security standards. This allows security officers to focus more attention on potential threats rather than routine identity checks.

Additionally, programs like Touchless ID show that AI surveillance does not always have to be harmful. When it is used in limited situations with clear rules and public knowledge, it is able to provide convenience while also protecting the safety of everyone. This shows that the issue is not necessarily the technology itself, but how it is being implemented.

Another example of the benefits of AI surveillance can be seen through the EuropeanUnion's Artificial Intelligence Act. Although the law itself places restrictions on certain AI practices, it still allows biometric identification systems to be used in specific situations when proper safeguards are in place (European Parliament 2024). The article states that “The use of biometric identification systems (RBI) by law enforcement is prohibited in principle, except in exhaustively listed and narrowly defined situations.” This shows that the lawmakers recognize that biometric identification systems can help improve security when they are being used responsibly and under strict regulations. Instead of putting a complete ban on the technology, the European Union allows its use in situations where it is able to help protect the people while still respecting citizens’ rights. Overall, both the TSA Touchless ID program and the European Union's AI Act show that AI surveillance and biometric identification systems can improve safety, strengthen the security measures, and also make identification processes more efficient when they are properly regulated and used responsibly.

The European Union’s approach shows that governments can support new ideas while still protecting citizens. Rather than banning AI technology entirely, lawmakers chose to establish rules that allow beneficial uses while limiting the dangerous ones. This balanced approach follows both the advantages and risks related with AI surveillance systems.

The Act also serves as an example for other countries that are trying to regulate the use of artificial intelligence. As AI technology continues to develop, governments around the world will need to create similar policies that encourage innovations while protecting individual rights (Richardson 2021). The European Union's regulations show that it is possible to benefit from AI technology without ignoring its possible risk.

AI surveillance can also help law enforcement locate missing people and respond to emergencies quickly. If it is used responsibly, facial recognition systems can help identify individuals that may be in danger and locate suspects involved in serious crimes. Supporters argue that these systems can provide valuable information in situations when the time is right. This shows that AI surveillance can be useful if applied carefully.

AI surveillance and biometric identification systems can provide a lot of benefits. There are still flaws to them. These technologies can improve security and make identification more efficient, but they can also make serious mistakes that can affect innocent people. In some cases, facial recognition systems have falsely identified individuals as criminals, while in others they have been used to unfairly target certain groups of people. These concerns demonstrate that AI surveillance technology can become harmful when it is wrong and biased.

AI surveillance systems can harm innocent people because facial recognition technology is not always accurate. One example of this can be seen in the case of Robert Williams, a Black man who was wrongfully arrested after a facial recognition system falsely identified him as a criminal.  During the investigation, detectives themselves admitted to their mistake by stating, “I guess the computer got it wrong” (Hill 2020). This evidence shows that facial recognition technology is not always reliable and that the mistakes made from AI systems can have serious consequences for innocent people. In Williams's case, he was arrested, fingerprinted, and jailed even though he had done nothing wrong. This shows how relying too heavily on AI technology can lead to wrongful accusations and violations of people's rights.

The Robert Williams case is important because it shows a real example of how mistakes made by AI systems can have real consequences for people. Williams was arrested in front of his family and treated as a criminal despite being completely innocent. Being falsely accused can ruin one's reputation forever, leaving a mark along with emotional stress, and reduce trust in law enforcement. Even after the mistake was discovered, Williams still had to deal with the effect of being wrongfully arrested. This shows that errors made by facial recognition systems are not just minor inconveniences but are serious events that have a serious impact on people's life.

William’s case also received national attention because it was one of the first well known examples of a wrongful arrest that was linked to facial recognition technology. The incident got a lot of attention and spared discussion about whether police departments should rely on AI systems during investigations. Many civil rights groups also argued that stronger safeguards were needed before the technology could be used as evidence against a suspect. This case brought attention to the risk and really encouraged lawmakers to examine the accuracy of the technology.

The case also raises questions about how law enforcement agencies use technologies during investigation. Facial recognition systems should be viewed as tools during investigation rather than systems that make decisions for them. Human judgement needs to be and should always be involved when it comes to determining whether someone is a suspect. If officials rely too much on technology without confirming the results, innocent people will continue to be harmed by mistakes made by AI systems.

Another concern when it comes to facial recognition systems is how often it affects minority groups. In the report AI-Enabled Surveillance Targeting Ethnic Minorities, Fergus Ryan and his coauthors explain that “Xinjiang authorities have blanketed the region in facial-recognition cameras, forced residents to install surveillance apps on their phones and implemented a ‘predictive policing’ regime that uses mass data collection and analysis to flag behaviour deemed suspicious” (Ryan et al. 2025, 50). This evidence shows how AI surveillance can be used to unfairly monitor and target specific groups of people. Although these systems can benefit people, they can also contribute to discrimination, loss of privacy, and government overreach (Turner Lee and Chin-Rothmann 2022).

The surveillance system in Xinjiang goes far beyond just the ordinary security measures. The residents are constantly being monitored through cameras, smartphones, and large databases created to collect personal information. The supporters of these systems argue that they are made to maintain order and stop crime. However, critics argue that such extensive monitoring removes personal privacy and gives the government too much control over people’s daily lives. This example is a demonstration of how AI surveillance can be used for purposes that go beyond public safety and could essentially become a tool for monitoring the entire population.

Furthermore, many people may not realize how much information is being collected about them. Facial recognition systems can track various different things such as where people go, who they interact with, and how they behave in public spaces. When that information is stored for a long period of time, it creates serious concerns about privacy and personal freedom. Without clear limits, governments and organizations have too much authority and can collect more information than is necessary, increasing the risk of misuse and abuse.

Another problem with AI surveillance is that many of the systems are trained to use large amounts of data that already contain bias. If biased data is being used to train an AI system, then the technology will continue to produce unfair results. Another problem with the Ai surveillance is that many of the systems are trained to use large amounts of the data that already contain bias. If biased data is being used to train an AI system then that technology will end up producing unfair results. In the Study “Gender Shades: Intersectional Accuracy Disparities in Commercial Gender Classification,”Joy Builamwini and Timnit Gebru found that the facial analysis systems had gender classification error  rate of up to 34.7% for darker skinned women, compared to a maximum of 0.8% for lighter skinned men (Buolamwini and Gebru 2018). The researchers found that the datasets that they examined contained mostly the lighter skinned individuals. This goes on to show how unequal the representation in the dataset was and how these differences in systems accuracy can contribute to the unfair treatment of certain groups. Similar differences in accuracy in the facial recognition systems could lead to certain groups being incorrectly identified more than the others (Grother et al. 2019). As a result, the exciting biases may become worse when AI systems are used without proper testing.

The Robert WIlliams case, the surveillance of ethnic minorities and the Buolamwini and Gebrus research both go on to show that the AI surveillance and facial analysis technologies can be inaccurate, biased and harmful when they are being used without proper safeguards.

Due to risks associated with AI surveillance, strict laws and multiple verification checks are necessary in order to protect citizens and prevent the misuse of these systems. One example of this can be seen in the European Union's Artificial Intelligence Act. The law places limits on facial recognition surveillance and bans certain AI practices that could harm citizens. According to the article, “The use of biometric identification systems (RBI) by law enforcement is prohibited in principle, except in exhaustively listed and narrowly defined situations” (European Parliament 2024). This shows that lawmakers understand that AI surveillance can be dangerous if left unchecked. By restricting when and how biometric identification systems can be used, the European Union is trying to prevent abuse while still allowing the technology to be used when necessary. These regulations are meant to help protect innocent people from being unfairly monitored or falsely identified.

Similar efforts to regulate facial recognition technology can also be seen in the US. In the article “Status of State Laws on Facial Recognition Surveillance: Continued Progress and Smart Innovations,” Jake Laperruque explains that by the end of 2024, fifteen states had already established laws limiting police use of facial recognition technology. Montana and Utah became the first states to establish warrant requirements for the police use of facial recognition, while Maryland limited its use to investigations involving certain serious crimes (Laperruque 2025). These regulations show how governments can continue to use AI surveillance to improve public safety while also placing limits on how the technology is being used. This can then help prevent the misuse of these systems while protecting the rights and the privacy of the citizens

Another reason strict safeguards are necessary can be found in Steven Feldstein's article "Distinguishing Between Legitimate and Unlawful Surveillance." Feldstein explains that legitimate surveillance requires “a robust, independent oversight system” that can authorize surveillance measures and provide remedies when abuses occur (Feldstein 2019a, 12). This shows that the surveillance systems should not be allowed to operate without supervision and full accountability. Instead of this governments need to create systems of oversight that ensure AI technologies are being used fairly and legally. Without these protections, AI surveillance can easily violate privacy and people rights and also lead to wrongful identification. Overall, both the EUAI Act and Feldstein's research show that strong laws, human oversight, and multiple verification checks are necessary to reduce wrongful identification and protect innocent people.

In addition to these strong laws, human verification should be required for major actions that are taken using AI surveillance systems. Facial recognition technology should never be used as the sole reason for an arrest, investigation or punishment. Instead, law enforcement must be required to confirm that the claim matches through additional pieces of evidence such as security footage, fingerprints, witness statements etc... Human review gives an extra layer of protection against mistakes and reduces the chances of innocent people being harmed due to technological errors.

Another example of the limitations of facial recognition technology can be seen in Margaret Hu’s article “Facial Recognition AI.” Here Hu explains that despite New York City having advanced surveillance systems, facial recognition AI failed to help the law enforcement correctly identify the suspect in the murder of UnitedHealthcare' s CEO in 2024 (Hu 2025). This shows that even the advanced AI surveillance systems have limitations and that they cannot always provide the information that the law enforcement needs. Therefore, law enforcement should continue to rely on human judgement and traditional investigation methods rather than just depending entirely on the AI technology.

Human verification is especially important because every situation is different. A facial recognition system may be able to compare images, but it can’t fully grasp the context and analyze in depth like a human can. Human reviewers can examine additional evidence, consider various different circumstances and determine whether the technology's conclusion makes sense. This additional step can prevent mistakes and improve fairness of the decision making process

Transparency is another really important safeguard. Citizens should have the right to know when AI systems are being used and how decisions are being made. When AI systems take part in decisions, people should be able to challenge those decisions as well as request an explanation for them. Greater transparency increases accountability and also helps ensure that organizations are using AI responsibly. Without being clear, error and biases may remain hidden from the public eye.

The importance of transparency can also be seen in the report “The Perpetual Line-Up: Unregulated Police Face Recognition in America.” The researchers found that out of 52 law enforcement agencies that had used facial recognition, only four had policies that were publicly available (Garvie et al. 2016). This shows just how little information was being shared with the public about the use of these systems at that time. Without access to these information, citizens would not know how their information was being collected or used. This is why it is important that law enforcement agencies should be required to make their policies available to the public and be held accountable for how they use these systems.

Transparency also helps build public trust in AI systems. People are more likely to accept new technologies when they understand how those technologies work and how their information is being used. If organizations keep their AI systems secret, citizens may become concerned about privacy violations and unfair treatment. Open communication helps reduce these concerns and encourages accountability.

In addition, transparency allows researchers and experts to be able to identify problems that lie within the AI system. When information about how systems operate is available, independent groups can then examine whether what the technology is producing is fair and has accurate results. This makes it easier to correct errors before they can affect the large population.

Furthermore, regular checks can ensure that AI systems are reliable, accurate and fair. Regular testing will allow experts to be able to identify bias, discrimination, and false identification before they cause harm. This also helps with the development and progress of these systems. If governments and companies are required to regularly evaluate their systems, that can improve accuracy while protecting the rights of the citizens. This creates a balance between benefiting from new technology and preventing its misuse.

Education is also very important when it comes to the responsible use of AI surveillance systems. Law enforcements, government officials and employees who use these technologies should be receiving proper training on their strengths and limitations. Understanding how AI systems work can help prevent people from relying on them too much and also reduce mistakes. When they get an understanding that AI is a tool rather than a perfect decision maker, they will be able to use it responsibly and efficiently.

Proper training can also help with recognizing the limitations of the AI systems. Many mistakes occur when people start to assume that technology is always correct. By educating people about errors such as bias and inaccuracy, the organization will lead to a better and more careful throughout decision making. This can reduce the chances of wrongful identification and improve the overall effectiveness of AI surveillance systems.

In conclusion, although AI surveillance and biometric identification systems are able to improve security and efficiency, they can also cause harm to innocent people if they are not carefully checked and looked after. As shown throughout this paper, AI systems can improve public safety and make identification processes faster, but facial recognition technology can also be inaccurate and biased toward the minority groups. Cases like Robert Williams's show the dangers of relying too much on AI without proper oversight. As technology becomes a larger part of everyday life, it is important to ensure that convenience and efficiency do not come at the cost of fairness, accuracy, and individual rights. Because of these risks, governments should create strict laws and require extra verification checks to protect people's rights. As AI technology continues to grow, society must balance safety and privacy by making sure these systems are carefully tested and regulated.
